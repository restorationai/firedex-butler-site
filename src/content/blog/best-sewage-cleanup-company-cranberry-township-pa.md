## What should you look for in a sewage cleanup company in Cranberry Township?

Five things separate a company that can safely close out a sewage job from one that just mops and leaves: 24/7 phone coverage, documented Category 3 handling, a written scope before tear-out, direct insurer communication, and an in-house rebuild crew.

Here's why each one matters:

- **24/7 answer.** Sewage doesn't wait for business hours, and the longer contaminated water sits on porous material, the more has to be removed instead of dried.
- **Category 3 protocol.** Any sewer or drain backup is black water under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry benchmark for how water losses get classified and handled. That classification drives what gets thrown out versus dried in place.
- **Written scope before tear-out.** You should see, in writing, what's being removed and why before a single piece of drywall comes down.
- **Works with your insurer.** Documentation (photos, moisture readings, a cause-of-loss note) is what gets a claim paid, not a verbal description after the fact.
- **In-house rebuild.** A company that only mitigates leaves you hunting for a second contractor to rebuild the basement they just gutted.

## Why does Category 3 protocol matter more than how fast someone shows up?

Speed matters, but handling the water correctly matters more, because a sewer backup is never clean water and treating it like a simple mop-up leaves contamination in the structure. Groundwater or sewage that enters through a floor drain or toilet, including anything from beyond the trap, is classified as Category 3 black water, full stop.

That classification changes the job. Porous materials that touched the water (carpet, carpet pad, drywall below the flood line, sometimes baseboard trim) typically get removed rather than dried, because they can't be reliably disinfected. The area then gets an antimicrobial treatment, and drying equipment runs on the structure that remains. A company that tries to dry Category 3 carpet instead of removing it is cutting a corner that shows up later as odor or a mold inspection you shouldn't have needed. ## Why do you need a written scope before any tear-out starts?

A written scope protects you because it documents exactly what's being removed, why, and at what approximate cost, before the crew starts cutting drywall. Without it, you have no record to compare against the invoice or to hand your adjuster.

Ask for photos of the affected area, moisture meter readings at the flood line, and a line-item list of what's coming out before work begins. FireDEX documents the scope this way specifically because it's what an adjuster needs to approve the claim without a second visit. ## Will homeowners insurance pay for sewage cleanup?

Only if your policy has a water backup (sewer/sump) endorsement; a standard homeowners policy usually excludes sewer backup and sump pump failure unless that endorsement was added. This is one of the most commonly misunderstood parts of a sewage claim.

Check your declarations page for a line labeled "water backup" or "sewer and drain backup" before you assume you're covered. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-language breakdown of what standard policies typically do and don't cover. If the endorsement is there, document everything before cleanup starts: photos, the cause (a backed-up line, a failed sump), and what got removed. That's the documentation FireDEX builds into the written scope above.
