**TL;DR:** If sewage is backing up through your bathtub or shower drain in Cranberry Township, stop using every drain and toilet in the house immediately and keep people and pets away from the bathroom. Call Cranberry Township's sanitary sewer department first to check the public main. If the main is clear, the blockage is on your side of the line and it's a plumber's job to clear it. Once the line is open, a restoration crew removes the contaminated materials and dries the structure, because this is Category 3 water under the IICRC S500 standard.

Water is coming up through the bathtub drain instead of going down it, and the toilet nearby gurgles every time someone flushes. That combination in an upstairs bathroom, whether it's a Fernway split-level or a two-story in Fox Run, almost always means the blockage sits downstream of the tub on the home's main drain line, not in the tub itself. The tub or shower is usually the lowest fixture on that branch, so it's the first place sewage shows up when something further down the line won't drain. Before you grab a mop, there are three calls to make, in order, and one mistake that makes the mess bigger.

## Why does sewage come up through the bathtub instead of the toilet?

The bathtub or shower drain sits at the lowest point on its branch of the drain-waste-vent system, so when something blocks the line downstream, wastewater finds the lowest exit before it finds the street. In a lot of Cranberry Township homes that's the tub, not the toilet.

Toilet gurgling at the same time is a second sign of the same blockage. It's trapped air escaping as water tries to push past the clog. If both fixtures are acting up together, the problem is almost always shared plumbing further down the line, not two separate issues.

## Who do I call first: the township, a plumber, or a restoration company?

Call Cranberry Township's sanitary sewer department first to rule out a problem with the public main before anyone touches the drain. If the township confirms the main is clear, call a licensed plumber to clear the blockage on your side of the line. Only after the line is flowing again does restoration work start.

Cranberry Township lists a daytime sanitary sewer line and a separate after-hours sewer emergency number on its public works pages; have both on hand, since a backup rarely waits for business hours. If the township traces the problem to your lateral or your home's internal plumbing, that's when a plumber snakes or jets the line.

Once the drain is running again, the cleanup is a separate job. That's where [FireDEX Butler](tel:+17244527400) comes in: the bathroom, the subfloor under the tub, and the ceiling of any room directly below it all need to be assessed, because sewage that sat on a bathroom floor often finds its way through grout lines and subfloor seams before anyone notices a stain downstairs.

## What should I do in the first few minutes, before anyone arrives?

Stop using every sink, toilet, washing machine, and dishwasher in the house until the blockage is confirmed and cleared. Running more water anywhere on the same line can push the backup further into the house.

Keep people and pets out of the affected bathroom and out of any room directly beneath it. Don't plunge or snake the tub drain yourself if you suspect a main-line or lateral blockage; pushing against a clog that's actually downstream can force contaminated water back up somewhere worse. If you see water pooling around the tub or on the bathroom floor, check the ceiling in the room below as soon as it's safe. Sewage water moves through subfloor and framing fast, and a ceiling stain forming an hour later is common.

## What happens once the drain line is clear?

Any water that came up through a tub, shower, or toilet drain is [Category 3 water](https://iicrc.org/s500/), also called black water, regardless of how clean it looks. That classification, laid out in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, means porous materials that got wet (bath mats, caulking, often the subfloor and baseboard) usually get removed rather than dried in place.

A restoration crew documents the affected area with photos before any demolition, pulls contaminated materials, treats the remaining structure with an antimicrobial, and sets drying equipment to bring moisture content back to normal before anything gets rebuilt. If odor lingers in the bathroom or hallway after the wet materials are gone, that's a separate pass with equipment built for it. You can see the full scope of what that work involves on our [sewage cleanup page](/services/sewage-cleanup/), and how Category 3 losses are handled generally in our [guide to Category 3 water damage](/blog/category-3-water-damage/).

A bathtub backup is a different animal from a [basement floor drain backing up](/blog/sewage-backup-basement-cranberry-township-who-to-call/), which more often points straight to the main lateral; either way, the materials and drying work follow the same Category 3 rules. If the ceiling below the bathroom got wet, that's [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) work layered on top of the sewage cleanup, not a separate call.

If you're standing in a bathroom right now watching water rise instead of drain, the order holds: shut off water use in the house, call the township to check the main, call a plumber if the main's clear, and have the contaminated materials assessed before you touch them yourself.
