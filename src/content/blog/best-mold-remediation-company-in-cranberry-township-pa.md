## What Should You Look for When Hiring a Mold Remediation Company?

The right company separates testing from remediation, gives you a written scope before demolition, and follows a recognized containment standard rather than just spraying bleach and moving on. Ask three things before you sign anything: who is doing the air or surface sampling, what containment method they use, and whether the moisture source will be fixed before the cleanup starts.

Mold remediation done without fixing the water problem first is wasted money. Growth typically returns within days once the underlying moisture is back, because mold can begin colonizing a wet surface in as little as 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home). That is why a company that can also handle [basement flooding cleanup](/services/basement-flooding-cleanup/) or [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) under the same scope of work is worth asking about, especially in Cranberry Township homes where sump-pump failures and foundation seepage are the usual root cause.

Ask whether containment follows the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/) for mold remediation, which governs how a crew isolates the work area, controls air pressure, and clears the space before removing containment. A company that references this standard, even without naming a specific certification, is working from an industry framework rather than improvising.

## How Does Mold Remediation Relate to Testing and Cost?

Testing and remediation are two different services, and a company that only offers one or the other may be sending you to a second vendor mid-project. [How to test for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/) walks through the difference between a DIY kit and a lab-backed inspection, and [signs of hidden mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) covers what to look for before you call anyone.

Cost varies with the square footage affected, how porous the damaged materials are, and whether containment needs to isolate an HVAC system. For a breakdown specific to this market, see [mold remediation cost in Cranberry Township](/blog/mold-remediation-cost-cranberry-township-pa/), which covers typical ranges and what drives the bill up or down.

Insurance rarely covers mold remediation as a standalone claim. Most homeowners policies limit or exclude mold coverage unless the growth traces directly back to a sudden, covered water loss, such as a burst pipe, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). A company that documents the moisture source alongside the mold scope gives you the best shot at any coverage that does apply.
