## Who Do You Call First: Pest Control or a Cleanup Company?

Call a licensed pest control company first if mice are still active in the home. A cleanup crew can remove contaminated material, but if the entry points are still open and food sources are still available, the droppings will reappear within weeks.

Pest control companies specialize in exclusion work: sealing entry points, placing traps or bait stations, and confirming the infestation is actually over. That is not something a restoration or biohazard cleanup company is licensed or equipped to do, and FireDEX does not perform pest control services. Once the exterminator confirms the mice are gone or the population is under control, that is the point to bring in a cleanup crew for the droppings, urine staining, and contaminated building materials.

If you are not sure which situation you are in, a quick way to tell: are you still hearing scratching in the walls or finding fresh droppings daily? That is still an active infestation, call pest control. If the droppings look old, dry, and the activity has stopped, that is a cleanup job. FireDEX's [biohazard cleanup](/services/biohazard-cleanup/) crew can usually tell the difference during an on-site assessment and will say plainly if pest control needs to go first.

## Why You Shouldn't Sweep or Vacuum Mouse Droppings Yourself

Dry sweeping or vacuuming rodent droppings can send contaminated dust into the air you breathe, which is the opposite of what you want. The CDC's cleanup guidance specifically recommends wetting droppings and nesting material with a disinfectant before any removal, not stirring them up dry.

The concern is hantavirus, a rare but serious illness linked to deer mice droppings, urine, and saliva. Breathing in contaminated dust is the main way people get exposed, which is exactly what a dry sweep or vacuum pass can do. Pennsylvania has had very few confirmed cases, and most mice carrying it are found in rural, rodent-dense areas rather than finished suburban basements. Still, the CDC's hantavirus guidance is clear that the safest approach to any accumulation of droppings is ventilate the space, wet it down, and let someone in proper PPE handle the removal rather than doing it with a broom and a dust mask.

If you already swept or vacuumed before reading this, don't panic. Ventilate the room, wash your hands, and avoid repeating it until the area has been properly disinfected.

## What About the Insulation, Drywall, and Odor Left Behind?

Contaminated attic insulation almost always needs to be removed rather than cleaned, since urine and droppings work down into fiberglass or cellulose fibers in a way that disinfectant alone can't fully reach. Drywall with visible staining or a strong, lingering odor usually comes out too.

The giveaway that odor removal is needed separately from the physical cleanup is a smell that persists after the visible material is gone. Rodent urine odor sits in wood grain and subfloor seams, and a hydroxyl or ozone treatment addresses that at the source rather than covering it with fragrance. Homeowners in older Zelienople and Butler housing stock with original wood subfloors tend to need this step more often than newer construction with sealed OSB subfloors.
