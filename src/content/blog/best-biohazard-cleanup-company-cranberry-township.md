## What should you look for before hiring a biohazard cleanup company?

A qualified biohazard cleanup company trains its technicians on OSHA's bloodborne pathogen standard, uses PPE rated for bodily fluid and pathogen exposure, and documents every step for your insurance adjuster and, when applicable, law enforcement or the medical examiner's office.

Biohazard cleanup covers unattended deaths, suicides, violent crime scenes, and situations involving blood or bodily fluids. It is regulated work, not a deep-clean service. OSHA's [bloodborne pathogen standard](https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens) sets the training and exposure-control requirements that apply to anyone working around blood or other potentially infectious materials, and a legitimate company can tell you, specifically, how its technicians meet that standard, not just that they are "trained."

Beyond training, look for a company that puts the scope of work in writing before cleanup starts: what gets removed, what gets disinfected and kept, and what the disposal process looks like. If a company can't answer those questions on the phone, that's a sign to keep calling.

## What questions should you ask before the crew arrives?

Ask these four questions before you agree to anything: who is actually on-site doing the work, how is the waste transported and disposed of, what does the written estimate include, and how will the crew handle access and parking to keep the situation private.

Specifically:

1. **Who performs the work?** Subcontracted crews with no direct accountability are common in this industry. Ask whether the technicians on-site are employees of the company you're talking to.
2. **What's the disposal process?** A real answer names a regulated medical waste hauler and a manifest trail, not a vague "we take care of it."
3. **What's in writing?** A scope of work should list affected materials, what's salvageable, and what has to be removed and replaced.
4. **How do you protect my privacy?** Ask directly how the crew handles vehicles, entry, and conversations with neighbors. A company that's thought about this will have a specific answer, not a scripted one.

## How is biohazard waste legally disposed of in Pennsylvania?

Biohazard waste in Pennsylvania has to be packaged, labeled, and transported as regulated medical waste, with a documented chain of custody from the property to an approved disposal facility.

That means contaminated materials get double-bagged or placed in rigid containers labeled for biohazard waste, then handed off to a licensed medical waste transporter rather than put out with household trash. A company doing this correctly can describe who their waste hauler is and show you the paperwork trail if you ask. If a company is vague about where the waste actually goes, that's worth pressing on, because improper disposal can create liability that lands back on the homeowner.

This is also where a written scope of work and insurance documentation matter together. Adjusters want to see that the job followed a defensible process, not just that a mess was cleaned up.
