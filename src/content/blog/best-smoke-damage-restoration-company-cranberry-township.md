## What should you ask before hiring a smoke damage restoration company in Cranberry Township?

Ask about six things before you sign a work order: how they assess the soot, their odor-removal method, whether they address the HVAC system, how they handle salvageable contents, whether the rebuild happens in-house, and whether they'll work your insurance paperwork directly.

- **Soot assessment**: dry soot (from a fast, hot fire) and wet soot (from a smoldering fire) need different cleaning agents and different surface treatments, so a company should name which type they're dealing with before they start.
- **Odor removal method**: look for a combination approach, source removal first, then HEPA air scrubbing and a sealed thermal fog or hydroxyl treatment, rather than a single air freshener pass. Our [guide to how professionals remove smoke odor](/blog/smoke-odor-removal-techniques/) covers why DIY attempts usually fail.
- **HVAC**: smoke particles travel through return air ducts and settle in the system. Ask whether duct cleaning or coil cleaning is part of the scope, since the [EPA's indoor air quality guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) flags HVAC systems as a common path for contaminants to recirculate through a home.
- **Contents**: ask whether they clean and store contents on-site or offsite, and how they document items before they're moved.
- **Rebuild in-house**: a company that frames, drywalls, and paints under one contract avoids the handoff gaps between a restoration crew and a separate contractor.
- **Insurance paperwork**: ask whether they write a line-item estimate before work starts and whether they'll communicate directly with your adjuster.

## How does a smoke and odor cleanup job usually unfold?

A smoke job moves through assessment, containment, cleaning, deodorization, and rebuild, usually in that order, with the odor step repeated until a clearance walkthrough passes.

First, the crew identifies the soot type and which surfaces are salvageable versus which materials (charred drywall, melted plastics, some insulation) need to come out. Then they set up HEPA air scrubbers to pull airborne particulate while they clean, working from ceiling to floor so cleaned surfaces don't get re-soiled by residue falling from above. Odor removal typically follows cleaning rather than masking it: a sealed thermal fog or hydroxyl generator treatment penetrates the same pores the smoke did, instead of just perfuming the air. Only after odor treatment is the space ready for reconstruction, new drywall, paint, and flooring where materials were removed.

## Will homeowners insurance cover smoke damage restoration in Cranberry Township?

Most standard homeowners policies cover smoke and soot damage from a sudden, accidental fire, since fire is a named peril on nearly every policy, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). Coverage details and deductibles still vary by carrier and policy.

Documentation protects the claim: photos of the damage before cleanup starts, a written itemized estimate, and receipts for any temporary repairs. Most adjusters expect mitigation to begin promptly, both to protect the claim and because smoke residue becomes harder to remove the longer it sits on finishes and grout lines, a point the [NFPA's home fire safety resources](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety) also touch on when discussing post-fire recovery.
