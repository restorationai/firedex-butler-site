## Call 911 or your utility company first if there's an immediate hazard

If you smell gas, see a downed power line, or have standing water anywhere near an electrical panel or outlet, that call comes before any other. No restoration crew, insurer, or contractor should walk into that situation first, and neither should you.

Downed lines are especially common after summer storm cells move through the Route 19/228 corridor and the I-79 interchange area, where mature trees line older streets. Treat every downed line as live until the utility company confirms otherwise, and keep people and pets away from standing water that touches it. Ready.gov has a short, practical rundown on severe weather hazards worth bookmarking before storm season, not during it.

Once the scene is safe, electrical and gas hazards are handled, nobody's trapped, no active fire, the order below applies.

## Who handles what after a storm: restoration company, insurer, or the township

A restoration company goes first because water and wind damage get worse by the hour, while an insurance claim can be filed once the property is stabilized. Calling the restoration crew first to tarp the roof or start extracting water, then your insurer, protects the home and still documents everything the claim needs.

Here's how the three usually divide:

- **Restoration company** (same day): emergency tarping over roof damage, board-up for broken windows, water extraction from a flooded basement or attic, and photo documentation of the damage as found.
- **Insurance company** (within 24 to 48 hours): you report the loss once mitigation is underway. Most homeowners policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, which is exactly what tarping and extraction do. Before you call, the [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) walks through what to document and photograph.
- **Cranberry Township** (as needed): downed trees or debris blocking a public road or right-of-way go to the township public works line, not your restoration company or insurer. Debris entirely on your own property is a cleanup and claims matter, not a township one.

## What if the roof is leaking right now?

A roof leak after a storm means water is actively entering the structure, and the priority is stopping it before it reaches drywall, insulation, and framing. Emergency tarping buys time until a roofer can make permanent repairs, and it's the difference between a ceiling stain and a full ceiling replacement.

Inside, place a bucket or tarp under the active drip, move furniture and electronics out of the path, and if water is coming through a light fixture, shut off power to that circuit before doing anything else. Document the leak with photos and a timestamp before you mop anything up. A restoration crew doing emergency board-up and tarping will cover the exterior opening first, then assess how far the water has already traveled into the ceiling cavity and attic insulation, which often goes further than the visible stain suggests.

If water has already dropped into a finished basement or lower level, that's a separate but related call, covered in detail in [who to call for water damage restoration in Cranberry Township](/blog/who-to-call-for-water-damage-restoration-in-cranberry-township-pa/).

## What happens when you call a restoration company

The first call is a short triage conversation: what's leaking or flooding, how long it's been happening, and whether anyone's in immediate danger. Have your address, a rough sense of which rooms are affected, and your insurance carrier's name ready, it speeds up both the dispatch and the eventual claim.

On arrival, the crew's first job is stopping the water, tarping a roof opening, boarding a broken window, or shutting down the source if it's an interior leak feeding into the storm damage. Next comes extraction of any standing water and a moisture check of walls, subfloor, and insulation, since storm water that looks dried on the surface can still be saturated underneath. Everything gets photographed and logged before demolition starts, which is the documentation your insurer will ask for.

For a wind or hail event that's left siding damage, gutter failure, or exterior debris alongside the leak, [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) covers the full scope, exterior stabilization through interior drying. If the active concern is specifically keeping water and weather out tonight, [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) is the narrower, faster service to ask for by name.

Once the property is stable, your insurer takes over the claims side, and that's the right order every time: safety, then stabilization, then the paperwork.
