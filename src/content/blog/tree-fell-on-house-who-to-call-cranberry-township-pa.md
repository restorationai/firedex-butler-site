**TL;DR:** If a tree just fell on your house in Cranberry Township, get everyone out of any room with a breached roof or wall and stay away from downed power lines. Call 911 only if someone is trapped or a line is sparking. After that, the order is: a licensed tree removal service to get the trunk and limbs off the structure, a restoration company to tarp the opening and stop water from getting in, then your insurance company to open the claim.

You need to know who picks up the phone next. Cranberry Township sits at the I-79/Turnpike crossroads with a lot of mature tree cover on older lots, and when a summer cell or a wind event drops a limb or a whole trunk onto a roof, the next two hours matter more than the next two days.

## Is it safe to stay in the house right now?

If the tree broke through the roof deck, cracked a ceiling joist, or came down near an electrical service line, treat the structure as compromised until someone qualified says otherwise. Don't walk directly under the damaged section and don't let kids or pets into that part of the house.

Look for sagging drywall, daylight visible through the ceiling, or a door and window that suddenly won't close right. Any of those mean the framing took real load. If the tree is leaning against the house rather than fully down, it can still shift. Stay clear of it and don't try to cut or move branches yourself.

Downed power lines are the other hard stop. If a line is touching the tree, the house, or the ground near it, stay back and call the power company's emergency line or 911. Never assume a line is dead because the lights are off.

## Who do you call first, second, and third?

The order is safety, then removal, then mitigation, then insurance, because each step depends on the one before it finishing.

1. **911 or the utility, if there's an immediate hazard.** Trapped occupants, sparking lines, gas odor, or active structural collapse come before any other call.
2. **A licensed tree removal company.** They have the rigging and chainsaw-on-a-roof experience to get the trunk and limbs off safely without doing more damage to what's left of the structure. A restoration crew should not be cutting a 60-foot oak off your roof, and a tree company shouldn't be drying out your attic.
3. **A restoration company, to tarp the opening and start drying out the water intrusion.** Once the tree is off, the hole it left is an open invitation for rain, and Western PA storms rarely come alone. This is the point where a company like [FireDEX Butler](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) gets involved, tarping the roof or boarding a broken window and wall section, then assessing how far water has already traveled into the attic insulation, ceiling drywall, or finished rooms below.
4. **Your insurance company**, after the opening is covered and you have photos of the damage. Calling the insurer before the roof is tarped risks more water getting in while you wait on hold; calling after means you're reporting a stabilized loss, not a still-growing one.

## What happens to the roof and the inside of the house once the tree is off?

The first job is sealing the hole, and the second is finding out how far the water already got. A tarp over a punctured roof section, secured with battens rather than just nails through the membrane, buys time until permanent repairs can be scheduled.

Inside, a tree strike almost never stays a roof problem. Water follows the path of least resistance down through attic insulation, along ceiling joists, and into the drywall and framing below. By the time you see a stain on a bedroom ceiling, insulation above it may already be saturated. That's the difference between a [tarping and board-up job](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) and a full [water damage](/services/water-damage-restoration/) mitigation: the second one includes pulling wet insulation, running air movers and dehumidifiers, and checking moisture levels in framing over several days so mold doesn't start in a cavity nobody can see.

If the strike also cracked rafters, splintered decking, or took out a section of exterior wall, that moves into [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) territory once the drying is done and the insurance scope is approved. Our [Cranberry Township storm damage guide](/blog/who-to-call-for-storm-damage-cranberry-township-pa/) walks through what that follow-up work typically looks like.

## Will homeowners insurance cover a tree that falls on your house, even if it's your neighbor's tree?

In most cases, your own homeowners policy pays for the damage regardless of whose tree it was, because standard policies cover wind and falling-object damage to your dwelling. The exception that trips people up is tree removal itself: many policies cap the amount they'll pay to remove the tree from the yard even though they'll cover the structural repair in full.

Whose insurance pays for removal and cleanup can get more nuanced if negligence is involved, for example a neighbor knew a tree was dead and didn't address it. That's a conversation for the adjusters, not something to sort out standing in the yard. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance) has a clear plain-language overview of what standard policies typically cover for storm and falling-object losses.

What protects your claim either way is documentation: photos of the tree, the point of impact, and the interior damage before anything gets moved or cut up, plus the tarping company's invoice showing when emergency mitigation started. Our [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) covers what to photograph and what to keep.

## What should you do while you wait for help to arrive?

Document what you can safely see from outside or from an unaffected room, move valuables and electronics out of any room below the damaged area, and set buckets or a tarp of your own over anything you can reach without climbing. Check the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/) for the hour-by-hour forecast so you know how much time you actually have before the next band of rain moves through Butler County.

Don't run a generator inside the garage or an enclosed space if the power is out, and don't attempt to climb onto the roof yourself. A tree strike plus a fall is a much worse Tuesday than a tree strike alone.

A fallen tree on the house is a three-call problem, not a one-call problem, but the order matters. Get the tree off safely, get the opening covered before the next storm cell, document everything along the way, and let the insurer step in once the bleeding has stopped.
