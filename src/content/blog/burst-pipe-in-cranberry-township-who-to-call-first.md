**TL;DR:** If a pipe just burst in your Cranberry Township home, shut off the main water valve and the electricity to any flooded area first. Then make two calls: a licensed plumber to stop the leak at its source, and a water damage restoration company to start extraction and drying. Drying needs to start within hours, not days, or the water damage turns into mold damage.

You're standing in water right now, maybe in a Fernway basement with galvanized supply lines finally giving out, maybe in a Seven Fields laundry room where a washer hose let go. Either way, the next ten minutes matter more than anything else today. Here's exactly what to do, in order, and who you actually need on the phone.

## What do you do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off your home's main water supply valve immediately, then cut power to any outlets or fixtures in the flooded area if you can reach the breaker panel without stepping in standing water.

The main shutoff is usually near your water meter, in the basement, a crawlspace, or where the supply line enters the house. If you don't know where it is, this is the moment to find out, because every minute the line stays open adds gallons to the loss. In Cranberry's older Fernway homes with aging galvanized plumbing, a split line can dump 10 gallons a minute or more.

Do not touch standing water near outlets, appliances, or an electrical panel. If the water is anywhere near wiring, leave the room and call an electrician or the fire department non-emergency line before you do anything else. Once the water's off and the area is safe, move anything you can lift off the floor: furniture, boxes, rugs.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

You need both, and the order depends on whether the leak is actually stopped. If you've shut off the main valve and the water has stopped, call the restoration company first, because drying has to start within hours. If water is still flowing anywhere, call a licensed plumber immediately to stop the source.

These are two different jobs. A plumber repairs or replaces the pipe, valve, or fitting that failed. A restoration company extracts the standing water, pulls out materials that can't be saved, sets up air movers and dehumidifiers, and documents everything for your insurance claim. One doesn't substitute for the other.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

The first 24 hours decide whether this stays a water job or becomes a mold job, so extraction and drying equipment typically goes in within hours of the call, not the next business day.

A restoration crew arrives with pumps and truck-mounted extractors to remove standing water first. Then they use moisture meters to map how far the water traveled behind walls, under flooring, and into baseboards, since visible wetness is almost never the full extent in a finished basement. Drywall, insulation, and flooring that stayed wet too long get removed; everything salvageable gets industrial air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to pull moisture out over the next three to five days, following drying targets consistent with the [S500](https://www.iicrc.org/) water damage standard.

Expect photos and moisture readings logged throughout. That documentation is what your insurance adjuster will actually look at, so a crew that tracks it from hour one saves you a second fight later.

## Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, including the drying, repairs, and removal of damaged materials, though they typically exclude the plumbing repair itself and any damage from a leak that was gradual or ignored.

Call your insurer after the water is shut off and mitigation has started, not before. Adjusters want to see that you took reasonable steps to limit the damage. Keep the plumber's invoice and the restoration company's scope of work; both get submitted with the claim. If the pipe failed because of long-term neglect, like a slow leak nobody addressed for months, the insurer may deny coverage for gradual damage even though the final burst was sudden. Our related [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) walks through documentation step by step if you want more detail before the adjuster calls.

Once the water's off and both calls are made, the rest is mostly waiting and checking in. Keep the dehumidifiers running, stay off freshly dried flooring, and let the crew finish their moisture readings before anyone starts rebuilding.
