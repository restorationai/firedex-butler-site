Wiping it with a dry rag or household cleaner usually smears it deeper into drywall and fabric, and painting over soot almost always lets the stain and the smell bleed back through within weeks.

## Who do I call first, 911 or a restoration company?

Call 911 and your local fire department first if there's still active fire, visible smoke, or you haven't been told it's safe to go back inside. Once the fire department or fire marshal releases the property, your next calls are your insurance company and then a smoke damage restoration company, in that order.

The fire marshal's job is determining cause and clearing the structure, not cleanup. If they issue an incident or case report, ask for the report number. Most insurers want it to open a claim. If the department shut off your gas or electric for safety, call the utility company directly to get it restored once the home is safe, that's not something a restoration crew handles.

Whether your home is a 1960s split-level off Fernway or a newer build near Graham Park, the sequence doesn't change: safety agencies first, insurer second, restoration crew third.

## What do I tell my insurance company before the restoration crew shows up?

Give your insurer the address, the fire department's incident number if you have one, and a plain description of which rooms were affected by flame, heat, or smoke. Ask directly whether your policy includes additional living expenses (ALE) if the home isn't livable right now.

Smoke damage from a covered fire loss is typically included under a standard homeowners policy, the same way the fire itself is (see the [Insurance Information Institute's overview of what's covered](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance)). Your insurer will usually want photos and a scope of work before repairs begin, which is part of what FireDEX documents on the initial visit. Keep receipts for anything you buy in the meantime, hotel nights, takeout, replacement filters.

## What actually happens when you call FireDEX Butler about smoke damage?

The first call is a triage conversation: which rooms were affected, whether the HVAC system was running during the fire, and whether windows, doors, or the roof need emergency board-up or tarping before weather or trespassers become a second problem. If the fire department broke a window or pulled a section of roof decking, that gets secured same-day, not left open overnight.

A technician then walks the property, checks soot penetration room by room (a fire in a kitchen can still leave residue in bedrooms if the HVAC was running), and documents everything for your claim before any cleaning starts. From there, the work usually follows HVAC and duct checks, content triage (what's cleanable versus what's a total loss), surface cleaning calibrated to the type of soot, and air scrubbing or deodorization to pull out the smell that soot leaves behind in fabric and porous material. ## Can I stay in my house while smoke damage is being cleaned up?

It depends on how far the smoke traveled and whether your HVAC system circulated it through the house. A fire contained to one room with the furnace or AC off at the time often leaves the rest of the home livable once that room is contained.

If smoke infiltrated the ductwork or multiple rooms, soot particles and odor compounds can keep circulating every time the system runs, which is a real indoor air quality concern, especially for kids, older adults, or anyone with asthma (the [EPA has guidance on indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) worth reading if you're unsure). A technician can tell you on the first visit whether the HVAC needs to stay off until it's cleaned, and whether staying elsewhere for a few days makes sense while air scrubbers run.

The [U.S. Fire Administration's guidance on recovering after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) is a good general reference while you're sorting out next steps, alongside your insurer and restoration crew.

If you're standing in a smoke-damaged room right now wondering where to start, the short version is: don't touch the soot, call your insurer to open the claim, and [schedule a smoke damage assessment](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) before anything gets wiped, painted, or thrown out.
