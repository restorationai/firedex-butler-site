**TL;DR:** If flash flood water is coming into your Cranberry Township home, cut power to any affected circuits first and stay out of the water. If the water is backing up from a storm drain or sewer, call the township's public works emergency line. Then call a water damage restoration company to start extraction, since flood water from outside is treated as contaminated Category 3 water. Call your insurer last, once mitigation is underway and you have photos.

## What should you do in the first 10 minutes?

Shut off electricity to any room with standing water at the breaker panel, not at the outlet, and keep everyone, especially kids and pets, out of the affected area until that's done. Never enter a flooded basement to flip a breaker yourself if the panel sits in or near the water.

After power is handled, move valuables and furniture off the floor if you can do it safely, and start taking photos and short videos of the water level, the source if it's visible, and anything damaged. This documentation matters later for your claim, and it costs you nothing to do while you wait for help.

If water is rising fast, rising above outlet height, or you smell sewage, leave the area and call for help rather than trying to manage it yourself. [Ready.gov's flood guidance](https://www.ready.gov/floods) covers the broader safety steps for a fast-moving flood event.

## Is floodwater in Cranberry Township safe to walk in?

No. Treat any floodwater that entered from outside, a storm drain, or a sewer line as Category 3, also called black water, under the IICRC's water damage standard. It can carry sewage, chemicals, and bacteria even if it looks clear.

Wading through it in shorts and sandals is a real health risk, particularly with any open cut or scrape. If the water came up through a floor drain or toilet, assume it's contaminated until a professional tells you otherwise. This is also why Category 3 losses usually mean removing soaked porous materials, like carpet pad and drywall, rather than just drying them in place.

## Who do you call first, the township or a restoration company?

Call the township first only if the problem is a storm drain backing up onto your street or a sewer main issue, not something inside your own plumbing. Cranberry Township's public works emergency lines are (724) 776-4806 during business hours and (724) 720-6464 after hours, and they're the right call for a blocked municipal storm drain or a street-level sewer backup affecting more than your property.

If the water is already inside your home, whether it came in around the foundation, up through a drain, or off the street, your next call is a water damage restoration company, not a plumber. A plumber can stop an active leak from a broken pipe or fixture, but flood water from outside needs extraction, moisture mapping, and often contamination cleanup, which is restoration work. ## What happens when you call a restoration company for flash flood cleanup?

The first call is a short triage conversation: where the water is coming from, how deep it is, and whether anyone has had contact with it. That tells the crew what equipment and PPE to bring before they're even on-site.

Once on-site, the crew extracts standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps, maps moisture into the walls and subfloor with a meter, and removes materials that can't be safely dried, like soaked carpet pad or drywall below the flood line. They'll document everything with photos as they go, which becomes the backbone of your insurance file. Full drying with air movers and dehumidifiers typically runs several days after extraction, depending on how much the structure absorbed; related work often falls under [flood damage restoration](/services/flood-damage-restoration/) or broader [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) depending on the source.

## When do you call your insurance company?

Call your insurer after the immediate hazard is handled and extraction has started, not before, since you have a duty to prevent further damage and a restoration crew already in motion demonstrates that. Have your policy number, photos, and the restoration company's initial scope ready when you call.

Here's the distinction that trips people up: a standard homeowners policy generally does not cover flood water that enters from outside, that requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) or a private flood carrier. A sewer or storm drain backup is also typically excluded unless you carry a water backup endorsement. Confirm with your agent which policy applies before you assume anything is covered.
