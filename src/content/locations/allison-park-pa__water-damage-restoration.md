## Why Allison Park Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Allison Park sits on the rolling terrain typical of Hampton Township and northern Allegheny County, where ranch and split-level homes were built into sloped lots rather than flat pads. That grading pattern is efficient for drainage in a light rain, but during the heavier spring thaws and summer downpours common to this part of western Pennsylvania, water finds the path of least resistance, usually straight toward a below-grade family room or finished basement. Add in the clay-heavy soil common across the region, which drains slowly and holds moisture against foundation walls longer than sandy soil would, and you get a steady stream of calls about seepage at the slab-wall joint, not just dramatic pipe bursts. Older homes in the area also tend to run original galvanized or early copper supply lines, which corrode from the inside and fail without warning, often behind finished walls where the leak goes undetected until flooring starts to cup.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Allison Park

The first visit is about finding every place water traveled, not just where it's visible. We pull baseboards and check wall cavities with moisture meters, because drywall can look dry at the surface while the stud bay behind it stays wet for days. Extraction comes next, using truck-mount or portable extractors depending on how much standing water there is and whether the home has the electrical capacity for heavy drying equipment. Structural drying follows a drying plan calibrated to the materials involved: engineered hardwood and OSB subfloor, both common in Allison Park's newer construction, dry differently than the plaster and full-dimension lumber found in the township's older farmhouses and bungalows. We monitor moisture content daily and document readings for your insurance file, drying to the standard outlined in IICRC S500 guidance rather than guessing at when a space feels dry.
